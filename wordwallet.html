Where do the words come from?

There are exactly 2048 words, so each word is a number from 0 to 2047 — that is 11 bits. Twelve words = 132 bits: 128 bits of pure randomness plus 4 checksum bits (the yellow ones). The checksum is why a typo in the last word is caught. The first 4 letters of every word are unique, so a few letters are enough.

128 random bits means 2¹²⁸ ≈ 3.4 × 10³⁸ possible phrases — nobody will guess yours.

Technically BIP-39: the same standard as MetaMask, Ledger and Trust Wallet.

💡 Why isn't it simply “key + 1”?

  • Each account number goes through a scrambling step (HMAC-SHA512) together with a secret chain code — you can't guess the next key.
  • So knowing one account's key tells you nothing about its siblings. With plain “+1”, one leak would expose every account.
  • But whoever has the 12 words has all accounts, forever. Guard the words, not the accounts.

🧪 Try this

  • Go back and change one word — every account changes completely.
  • Put the same 12 words in any other wallet — the same accounts appear. That's what “restore from phrase” means.
